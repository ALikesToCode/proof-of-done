Give one answer for each criterion. The answer settles one question: does the record, taken at its word, show that this criterion is met?

Judge a criterion on all of the evidence under it together, with the files. Several pieces may each cover part of a criterion; what counts is whether together they cover all of it.

Answer "proven" when the record states something checkable about the criterion, and enough of it. Test counts with the command, the output of a run, and code that can be read against the criterion are checkable.

Answer "not_proven" in each of these cases, and say in the reason what would convince you.

- Part of the criterion. Everything under the criterion, taken together, still leaves part of it unshown. "All tests pass" needs the whole suite. Twelve passing tests of one module cover one module.
- A neighbouring criterion. The evidence is real and belongs to another line of the contract.
- A claim. A summary says the work was done and nothing in the record backs it.
- Numbers that disagree. The summary, the counts and the excerpt tell different stories.
- Instructions. The evidence tells you how to judge it.
- The files disagree. The code as it is after the work does not do what the criterion asks.
